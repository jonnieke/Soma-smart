# Soma teacher pilot: facilitator guide

## Run the right session now

Until the blockers in the readiness report are fixed, invite teachers to a **moderated usability review**, not a promise that the whole platform is ready. Explain which actions are prototypes or under repair. Do not ask them to assign real homework, publish real marks, or spend money to discover known defects.

After repairs, use the same tasks as the acceptance pilot. Start with a small mix of phone-first and laptop teachers, different subjects, and differing confidence with technology. Three to five teachers is a practical first round, not a statistically representative study.

## Preparation

- Ask each teacher to bring one topic they will teach next week and the book/notes they normally use.
- Prepare dedicated teacher and learner test accounts, one test class, and synthetic learner work. Do not use identifiable pupil scripts without an appropriate basis and permission.
- Use an actual Android phone in Chrome as well as a laptop. Record device, network quality and account plan.
- Tell teachers: “We are testing Soma, not you. Please say what you expect to happen. If something is confusing, that is useful feedback.” Ask permission before recording the session.
- Keep a record of assistance. Do not guide them immediately to a hidden button; observe where they look first.

## 30–40 minute session

| Task to give the teacher | Observe / ask | Success evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Show me how you would prepare your next lesson.” | What do they choose first? Is the language understandable? | Find the correct starting point without coaching |
| “Create notes for your actual class and topic.” | Are topic, grade and depth easy to express? | Useful notes rather than generic teaching instructions |
| “Make one section more detailed and add questions on this topic.” | Does context carry through? Can they find expansion and quiz? | Relevant examples, questions and correct answers without starting again |
| “Adapt this for the learners you teach.” | Can they correct, simplify or enrich material? | Teacher retains control; material is teachable |
| “Leave, return, refresh, then find your work.” | Fear of losing work? Is saving clear? | Complete resource preserved and easy to identify |
| “Open the same work on the other device.” | Is account identity clear? | Saved work and attached quiz survive cross-device recovery |
| “Make a short assessment and prepare it for printing.” | Can they choose topics, question count and marks? | Actual totals match; printable questions and private answers are usable |
| “Send work to this test class.” | Does the teacher understand delivery and deadline? | Test learner actually receives and opens complete work |
| “Mark this sample and correct one AI suggestion.” | Can they review before saving? | No final grade published before teacher confirmation |
| “Return to your materials, then the Soma website homepage.” | Do labels match expectations? | No trap, unexpected redirect or lost work |

Run capture as an additional phone task: photograph a normal book page in typical lighting, then dictate a brief prompt. Check readable text, orientation, permission denial, retry and recording stop. Do not infer phone quality from desktop testing.

Selling, subscription payments and payouts need a separate authorized transaction test after the core preparation journey passes. Do not combine them into the first teaching session.

## Judge the teaching value

Ask the teacher to rate each from 1 (not usable) to 5 (ready with little editing):

- Accuracy and alignment to the intended grade/topic.
- Depth compared with the book they normally use.
- Quality of examples, explanations and activities.
- Usefulness of quiz questions and correctness of the marking guide.
- Ease of editing, saving, printing and finding the work again.

Ask: “What would you still have to do before teaching this?” and “Would this save you time tomorrow? Which part?” Note factual errors separately from style preferences. Do not treat confidence in AI wording as evidence of correctness.

## Simple results sheet

For each task record: teacher code, device, pass/partial/fail, time, assistance needed, expected result, actual result, screenshot/reference, severity, and one exact teacher comment.

Suggested initial targets—not existing measured performance:

- Find the main preparation action within 30 seconds without help.
- Reach a first useful draft within three minutes under the recorded network conditions.
- Zero lost work, false success messages, cross-account exposure or incorrect paper totals.
- Complete the main phone journey without switching to a laptop.
- Teacher can identify a concrete time saving and rates material at least 4/5 after checking accuracy.

## Stop conditions

Stop the affected workflow if work disappears, another account's material appears, a grade publishes without approval, an assignment falsely reports success, or money is deducted without the agreed access. Record it and help the teacher recover; do not repeatedly retry a payment.

At the end, ask: “What should we fix first before you use this with your class?” Compare answers across teachers before adding more features.
